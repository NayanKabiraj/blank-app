# Viral playbook: topics that spread, scripts people finish

Used by the daily Shorts task (`shorts/CLAUDE_DAILY_TASK.md`). The task file holds the scoring
rubric and the scene curve; this file holds the thinking and the examples. None of this is a
guarantee, because nobody can promise a Short will go viral. It is a checklist that stacks the odds:
a question people already wonder about, a first line that stops the swipe, and a payoff that earns
the share.

## Why people finish a Short and share it

- **Curiosity gap.** The viewer must feel a specific piece of knowledge is missing. "How does
  noise cancelling work?" is a topic; "Your headphones fight noise with more noise" is an itch.
- **Open loop, then payoff.** Ask in the first line, answer in the last third. Every scene adds
  one new reason to stay, and the answer arrives only when the viewer is sure to be listening.
- **Surprise.** One fact that makes someone say "wait, really?" is worth more than five that are
  merely correct. Check it, then lead with it.
- **Identity and utility.** People share what makes them look smart or helps a friend ("now I can
  explain why the sky is blue"). Make the payoff short and quotable.
- **Replay.** If the last line echoes the first, the Short loops and replays count as extra watch time.

## Picking the topic

Good signs: billions have seen it, almost nobody can explain it, and a number or contrast makes it
vivid. Strong families that fit this channel:
- *Secret behind a daily gadget or service*: UPI, face unlock, GPS, Wi-Fi, QR codes, the fridge, chai
  boiling over, why phones heat up.
- *Everyday mysteries of the body and mind*: why we yawn, why time flies, why we dream, why we get goosebumps.
- *Nature and weather you can see this week*: the monsoon, fog, a meteor shower, why winter feels dry.
- *Scale and speed that break intuition*: "faster than a jet", "heavier than...", "older than...".
- *India-first angles*: ISRO launches, railways and metro, cricket physics, festivals (Diwali firecracker
  colours, Holi colours), monsoon, Aadhaar and UPI. They make local viewers feel seen, and the science stays universal.
- *Timely hooks*: tie a question to something in the next seven days so it rides search and
  conversation spikes. Always confirm the event and dates from a reliable source.

Skip: breaking tragedies, politics and elections, medical or investment advice, promotion of a brand,
private individuals, claims you cannot confirm from two reliable sources, and anything whose honest
answer is "nobody knows".

## Hook formulas (first sentence, 14 words or fewer)

| Formula | English | Hindi (native, not translated) |
|---|---|---|
| Impossible-sounding fact | "A day on Venus is longer than its year." | "शुक्र ग्रह पर एक दिन, एक साल से लंबा होता है।" |
| You already live this | "Your phone knows it's you, even in the dark." | "अंधेरे में भी आपका फ़ोन आपको पहचान लेता है।" |
| Specific number | "Two seconds. That's all UPI needs to move your money." | "सिर्फ़ दो सेकंड में UPI आपका पैसा भेज देता है।" |
| Contradiction | "Sunlight is white. Space is black. So why is the sky blue?" | "धूप सफ़ेद है, अंतरिक्ष काला, तो आसमान नीला क्यों?" |
| Challenge | "Touch a steel spoon, then a wooden one. Same room. Why does steel feel colder?" | "स्टील की चम्मच छुएँ, फिर लकड़ी की। एक ही कमरा, तो स्टील ठंडी क्यों?" |
| Reversal | "Shooting stars are not stars. They're dust." | "टूटते तारे, तारे नहीं हैं। वे धूल हैं।" |

Never open with: "Today", "In this video", "Hello", "Welcome", "Did you know", "Have you ever wondered",
a definition, or the channel name. Start in the middle of the thought.

## Re-hook patterns (use one every 8–10 seconds)

- A turn: "But there's a catch." / "Now it gets strange." / "Then someone checked." / "Here's the twist."
- A question the last sentence raised: "So how does it know?" / "But why only at night?"
- A number that reframes: "That's more than two hundred times faster than a jet."
- A contrast: "Steel and wood. Same temperature. Completely different feel."
- A callback to the hook's own words, to remind the viewer what they are waiting for.

## Payoff and loop

- The payoff is one sentence of 12 words or fewer, built from the hook's own words.
  Hook: "So why is the sky blue?" Payoff: "The sky is blue because air scatters blue light the most."
- The last line before the follow call echoes the hook so the loop is clean: hook "Sunlight is white.
  Space is black." / close "White light in, blue sky out."
- The follow line stays short and last. Nothing after it.

## Title and hook text

- Title: curiosity question or bold claim, key word in the first 40 characters, 60 characters or
  fewer when you can. Patterns that work: "Why X does Y (it isn't what you think)", "The real reason X",
  "How X actually works", a number in the title. The video must answer the title completely.
- `hook_text` (burned in for the first three seconds): 5 words or fewer, a teaser, not a summary
  ("It sees you in the dark", "These aren't stars").
- Description line one: restate the question and tease the answer, then hashtags at the end.

## Self-check before starting the workflow

1. Does the first sentence work with the sound off and in under four seconds?
2. If I deleted the second sentence of any scene, would the viewer still want the next scene?
3. Is the most surprising number around two thirds in, and does it show as a countable difference?
4. Could someone say the payoff in one sentence to a friend?
5. Does the last line echo the first?
6. Is every fact confirmed from reliable sources, and is every number spelled as the voice should say it?
