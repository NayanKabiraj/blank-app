# Script patterns, with examples

The examples quoted below are narration from videos made in the Grow With Kaz tutorials
(Nov 2026). They're here to show technique. Don't reuse their wording or topics as they are.

## 1. Surprising-claim opener
> "For years, one number supposedly explained how much money you needed to be happy.
> Seventy-five thousand dollars a year."

> "One of the most feared hunters of its world opened its mouth. And there were no teeth
> inside. There were blades."

Pattern: *[widely believed / dramatic setup] + [short sentence that flips it]*. The flip
sentence is short (3–6 words) and gets the scene's biggest visual moment.

## 2. Time-and-place establishing line
> "About three hundred and sixty million years ago, in a warm, shallow sea that once
> covered what is now Ohio…"

Pattern: *[when] + [where] + [one sensory detail]*. Good for history, nature, space and
inventions. Skip it for everyday tech, where "right now, in your pocket…" does the same job.

## 3. Dry wit that lands the point
> "Below that, more money helped. Above it, happiness apparently stopped improving.
> Congratulations, your emotions had a salary cap."

> "You can be proud of your career while spending Tuesday arguing with a printer."

Pattern: restate the idea as an absurd, everyday consequence. Use one per Short, at most.
Keep it kind and never aim it at a group of people.

## 4. "Then someone checked" twist
> "Then another researcher checked. His results disagreed. So he teamed up with one of the
> scientists behind the original finding…"

> "Early reconstructions imagined a sleek shark-like giant up to ten meters long. Then in
> twenty twenty-three, a study compared head proportions across nearly a thousand species…"

Pattern: *old belief → who re-checked, and when → what they found → why it matters*. It
makes a fact into a story and gives the middle of the video a reason to keep watching.

## 5. Term + definition in the same breath
> "This is Dunkleosteus, an armored fish from a group called placoderms."

Never leave a technical word for the viewer to look up.

## 6. Physical analogy
> "Its head and the front of its body were wrapped in thick plates of bone, like a knight's
> helmet fused to a chest plate."

Pattern: *[part] + like + [familiar object, slightly unexpected]*. A good analogy can be
drawn as a prop.

## Applying it to a 50-second Short (worked outline)

Topic: "How do noise-cancelling headphones erase sound?"

| Beat | Line | Graphic |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (claim + flip) | "Your headphones fight noise with more noise. And it works." | `title`, prop `bulb` |
| Short answer + term | "It's called active noise cancelling: playing the exact opposite of a sound." | `keyword` |
| Wit | "Basically, your earbuds are arguing with the aeroplane, and winning." | `keyword`, prop `brain` |
| Mechanism | "A tiny mic listens. A chip flips the wave. The speaker plays the flipped copy." | `steps` |
| Twist (then someone checked) | "Paul Lueg patented the idea in nineteen thirty-six, decades before chips were fast enough to do it." | `stat` "1936" |
| Analogy | "Two kids pushing a swing from opposite sides: it doesn't move." | `compare` |
| Close | "Best on steady hums. Follow to understand one new thing every day." | `title` |
