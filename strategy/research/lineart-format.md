# Line-art story Shorts: what suits the format (research, 28 Nov 2026)

Status: PARTIALLY VERIFIED. Direct data on line-art channels was NOT obtainable: vidIQ credits were exhausted (3 left, calls cost 5) and web search returned vendor and SEO pages. What is below is what the sources support plus the channel's own numbers; it is a hypothesis to test, not a result.

## Evidence
- YouTube's own Culture & Trends blog describes "storytime" animation as minimally animated storytelling with a narrator, an established format (https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/line-art/ and /how-i-represent-storytime-swoozie). VERIFIED that the format exists; no performance numbers.
- Creative Bloq on single-line animation (https://www.creativebloq.com/art/2d-animation/im-mesmerised-by-these-clever-line-art-animations): short, looping, minimal line animations are admired; the examples are wildlife and everyday-motion pieces. Vendor and template pages say symbolism and emotional arcs suit black-line animation (commercial sources, weak evidence).
- Shorts guides (mediacube, kineclip, korpi; SEO blogs, weak evidence): narrative/storytelling Shorts rank among the better niches, tension holds viewers to the end, a hook in the first 3 s and a looping or earned ending help; Shorts RPM is tiny, long-form carries the money.
- YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" clarification (https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/): mass-produced, near-identical narrated stories or slideshows are ineligible for monetization; faceless is not itself penalized. Consequence for us: every line-art story must have its own hand-written scene and plot, never one template with the words swapped.
- Our channel (shorts.patterns, 28 Nov): story family 1.22x the median views per day on 12 videos with 58 % average viewed (space+nature 1.27x, 56 %); the old code-made horror stories were Part-2 candidates (25/day and 19/day). Small samples.

## What suits (decisions)
1. **Short invented stories with a turn** (fable / gentle tale / quiet mystery), 40-55 s, one character and one place, a hook that is a concrete image or paradox, one open loop, a reversal, an implied ending. Line art draws characters and small actions well; it does not do big spectacle or dense data.
2. **Day-to-night or place-in-time arcs**: the library's strength is the time-of-day palette, lights coming on, walking and carrying things; stories should use it (lamps, windows, a lantern, weather).
3. **Not for**: facts-heavy explainers (the vox Manim packs do those better), gore, anything needing realism, long documentaries (render cost).
4. **Cadence**: Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday replace the explore slot (so the upload total stays at 6); judge after 6 videos on views per day vs the channel median and average viewed %; keep, change or drop.
5. **Series**: "Ash Lane" (same town, new tales: the clockmaker, the ferryman, the baker). A recurring town gives returning viewers a reason to subscribe without reusing a plot.
6. **Hindi**: same story, Hindi narration, same scene (timing follows the voice).
